How does pressure affect enthalpy H? As we showed above we have the following relations of first and second order for G

((∂G)/(∂T))_P = −S

((∂G)/(∂P))_T = V

[Note: corrected; the source prints "((∂G)/(∂P))_T = −V" where this reads "((∂G)/(∂P))_T = V". From dG = VdP − SdT.]

−((∂S)/(∂P))_T = ((∂V)/(∂T))_P

We also know that by definition:

G = H − TS

Consider an isothermal change in pressure, so taking the partial derivative of each side of Equation (def), we get:

((∂G)/(∂P))_T = ((∂H)/(∂P))_T − T((∂S)/(∂P))_T

((∂H)/(∂P))_T = V − T((∂V)/(∂T))_P

For an ideal gas

((∂V)/(∂T))_P = nR/P

[Note: corrected; the source prints "(∂V)/(∂T) = nR/P" where this reads "((∂V)/(∂T))_P = nR/P". At constant P.]

so Equation (Eq12) becomes

((∂H)/(∂P))_T = V − T(nR/P) = 0

As we can see for an ideal gas, there is no dependence of H on P.

### Infrared Spectroscopy

Ethers are difficult to identify by IR spectroscopy. Although they show an absorption due to C–O single-bond stretching in the range 1050 to 1150 cm^–1, many other kinds of absorptions occur in the same range. Figure 18.8.1 shows the IR spectrum of diethyl ether and identifies the C–O stretch. Phenyl alkyl ethers show two strong absorbances for C–O stretching at 1050 and 1250 cm^–1. Figure 18.8.2 shows the IR spectrum of anisole.

Figure 18.8.1: The infrared spectrum of diethyl ether, CH3CH2OCH2CH3.
An infrared spectrum with peaks around around 3000 centimeters inverse, and around 1200-1100 centimeters inverse, labeled C-O stretch.

Figure 18.8.2: The infrared spectrum of anisole.
An infrared spectrum with peaks (in wavenumbers) above and below 3000, labeled s p 2 and s p 3 C H respectively, peaks around 1500-1600 labeled aromatic C C.

### Nuclear Magnetic Resonance Spectroscopy

Hydrogens on carbon next to an ether oxygen are shifted downfield from the normal alkane resonance and show ^1H NMR absorptions in the region 3.4 to 4.5 δ. This downfield shift is clearly seen in the spectrum of dipropyl ether shown in Figure 18.8.3.

Figure 18.8.3: The ^1H NMR spectrum of dipropyl ether. Protons on carbon next to oxygen are shifted downfield to 3.4 δ.
Proton N M R with shifts at 0 (TMS), 0.92 (triplet), 1.58 (sextet), and 3.36 (triplet). Relative areas are 1.50, 1.00, and 1.00 respectively.

Epoxides absorb at a slightly higher field than other ethers and show characteristic resonances at 2.5 to 3.5 δ in their ^1H NMR spectra, as indicated for 1,2-epoxypropane in Figure 18.8.4. The methylene protons of this epoxide are diastereotopic, and display complex splitting (see Section 13.7 and Section 13.8).

Figure 18.8.4: The ^1H NMR spectrum of 1,2-epoxypropane.
Proton N M R with shifts at 0 (TMS), 1.32 (doublet), 2.42 (doublet), 2.75 (doublet), and 2.97 (multiplet). Relative areas are 3.00, 1.00, 1.00, and 1.00 respectively.

Ether carbon atoms also exhibit a downfield shift in the ^13C NMR spectrum, where they usually absorb in the 50 to 80 δ range. For example, the carbon atoms next to oxygen in methyl propyl ether absorb at 58.5 and 74.8 δ. Similarly, the methyl carbon in anisole absorbs at 54.8 δ.
